How We Score This

No black-box third-party risk score — every point is explainable. Each item's 0–100 Lifecycle Risk Score is built from three weighted components:

  • End-of-life status (up to 55 pts) — pulled live from endoflife.date's public database where the product is tracked. Scales with years past EOL — the longer it's been end-of-life, the higher the points.
  • Security exposure (up to 25 pts) — a live CVE check via OSV.dev where applicable, plus a flat uplift whenever a product is EOL. That uplift exists because new CVEs stop being filed for EOL components — the silence is risk, not safety.
  • Support availability (up to 10 pts) — whether paid extended support is even an option, or the product is fully abandoned.
  • Criticality multiplier (1.0× – 1.3×) — applied based on how many of your apps depend on that one component.

Products not in the free public EOL database (common for older enterprise COTS like Oracle Forms, WebLogic, or Documentum) get an AI-generated estimate instead of a live lookup — and every result is labeled verified or ai-estimate so you always know which is which.

⚠ Important disclaimer

This is an educated estimate, not an audit. EOL dates are pulled from a live public database and are generally reliable; CVE counts are best-effort and skew toward open-source package ecosystems rather than enterprise COTS; AI-estimated items and the entire cost/liability range are directional, not precise. Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a "clean" scan from my CVE tool still show up risky here?

Once a product goes end-of-life, there's no maintainer left to coordinate CVE disclosure — new vulnerabilities in EOL software are often simply never filed. A standard dependency scanner reads that silence as "no known issues." This tool treats it as exactly the opposite: EOL status carries the largest weight in the score regardless of what a CVE count shows.

What counts as a "product" I should enter?

Anything with its own release/support lifecycle: operating systems, language runtimes (Java, .NET, Python, PHP, Ruby), web frameworks (Angular, React, Rails, Laravel, Spring), databases, web/app servers, and enterprise COTS platforms (Oracle, SAP, Documentum, WebLogic). Click a quick-add chip above to see the expected format.

What if my product isn't recognized?

Try the most common/generic name for it (e.g. "PostgreSQL" rather than a specific distribution's packaging name). If it's a well-known enterprise product not in the free public EOL database, the tool asks AI to generate an estimate instead — clearly labeled as an estimate, not a verified lookup.

Is this a replacement for a real technical audit?

No. It's a free, directional starting point using public data and your own inputs. A real technical due diligence or modernization assessment reviews your actual codebase, contracts, and infrastructure — book a discovery call if that's the next step.

Do you store what I enter?

No code, no SBOM, and no system access are requested in this version — just product names and versions you type in, sent to our serverless function to run the live lookups and generate your result. Nothing is stored unless you explicitly ask to have the report emailed to you.
