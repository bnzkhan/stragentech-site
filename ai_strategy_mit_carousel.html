01 | Business Purpose
What business problem are you actually trying to solve?
Don't start with the model—start with the strategy. AI should fix operational bottlenecks, scale revenue, or elevate customer experience, not just fulfill an innovation metric[cite: 2].
💡 Action: Identify top-tier business bottlenecks before evaluating vendor tools[cite: 2].

02 | Data Readiness
Is your data infrastructure ready to support AI?
Garbage in, garbage out. High-performing AI models require clean, structured, accessible, and secure data pipelines[cite: 2].
💡 Action: Audit data accessibility, governance, and security early[cite: 2].

03 | People & Culture
Do you have the right talent and culture in place?
AI execution is 20% technology and 80% change management. Success demands cross-functional collaboration and a culture open to continuous adaptation[cite: 2].
💡 Action: Map internal skill gaps and prioritize change enablement early[cite: 2].

04 | Workflow Integration
How will AI seamlessly fit into daily workflows?
The best AI tool is useless if adoption friction is too high. AI must augment and streamline current processes rather than adding extra operational steps[cite: 2].
💡 Action: Design human-centric workflows around existing team routines[cite: 2].

05 | Risk & Governance
How will you manage AI risk, ethics, and compliance?
AI introduces unique risks regarding data privacy, algorithmic bias, IP exposure, and reliability. Governance cannot be treated as an afterthought[cite: 2].
💡 Action: Establish robust guardrails and human-in-the-loop validation[cite: 2].
AI Strategy Playbook Source: MIT Sloan School of Management
06 | ROI & Value
How will you measure success and return on investment?
Avoid vanity metrics. Track tangible operational efficiency, cost reductions, speed-to-market, or top-line growth tied directly to AI initiatives[cite: 2].
💡 Action: Define baseline metrics and value milestones before launching pilots[cite: 2].
