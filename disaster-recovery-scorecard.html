How We Score This

Twelve questions, two per dimension, each scored on a 0–3 maturity scale and interpreted by AI against a model built from established disaster recovery and business continuity frameworks — not a generic checklist:

  • Business Impact & Recovery Objectives — whether RTOs/RPOs are defined and grounded in an actual Business Impact Analysis, per NIST SP 800-34's contingency planning process.
  • Backup & Data Resilience — immutability, air-gapping, and restore testing, given how routinely backup repositories are the attacker's first target (see stats above).
  • Infrastructure & Redundancy — single points of failure and failover design, adapted from the fault-isolation and redundancy practices in AWS's Well-Architected Framework Reliability Pillar — the same lens applies whether you're on AWS, another cloud, or on-premises.
  • Documentation & Runbooks and Testing & Exercises — whether a plan exists independent of any one person's memory, and whether it's actually been exercised — the plan-development and testing/maintenance stages of the NIST contingency planning lifecycle.
  • Governance, Communication & Compliance — clear incident roles and escalation paths, aligned with the business continuity management system approach in ISO 22301.

The four maturity levels (Ad Hoc, Developing, Standardized, Resilient) are a simplified version of the five-level Ad Hoc → Fragmented → Standardized → Proactive → Adaptive progression commonly used in industry DR maturity models.

⚠ Important disclaimer

This is a directional self-assessment, not a formal audit. The AI interpretation is based entirely on your own answers — there's no independent verification of your actual infrastructure, backups, or documentation. Treat the score and narrative as a prioritization aid, not a certification of compliance or resilience.

If recovery objectives, compliance posture, or cyber insurance requirements are on the line, pair this with a formal Business Impact Analysis and DR test — not a substitute for one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is this different from just asking "do you have a DR plan"?

Most informal checks stop at "yes/no, we have a plan." This scorecard breaks disaster recovery into six dimensions that tend to fail independently of each other — you can have a written plan and still have untested restores, or tested restores and still have a single person who's the only one who knows how to execute them. The AI report is built to surface exactly which of those gaps is yours.

What do the maturity levels mean?

Ad Hoc — recovery depends on improvisation and tribal knowledge in the moment. Developing — some pieces exist but are inconsistent or undocumented. Standardized — documented, repeatable processes exist for critical systems. Resilient — tested, redundant, and not dependent on any single person, system, or site. These map onto the commonly used Ad Hoc → Fragmented → Standardized → Proactive → Adaptive industry progression, condensed to four tiers here.

Is this specific to cloud, on-premises, or hybrid environments?

Neither — the six dimensions (objectives, backups, redundancy, documentation, testing, governance) apply regardless of where your infrastructure actually lives. A few questions mention cloud concepts like multi-region failover, but the underlying maturity question — "is there a fallback, and has it been tested?" — is the same whether that fallback is a second AWS region or a second physical site.

Is this a replacement for a formal DR audit or Business Impact Analysis?

No. It's a free, directional starting point you can complete in a few minutes using only what you already know. A real Business Impact Analysis or DR audit reviews your actual systems, backup logs, and recovery test evidence — book a discovery call if that's the next step.

Do you store what I enter?

No. Your answers are sent to our AI service solely to generate your report and are not stored. Nothing is saved anywhere unless you explicitly ask to have the report emailed to you.
