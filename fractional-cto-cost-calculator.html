How We Calculate This

Three independent 12-month totals, each built from the inputs above:

  • Full-Time CTO = (base salary × (1 + benefits/overhead %)) + recruiting cost + (cost of delay × months to fill).
  • Fractional CTO = monthly retainer × 12.
  • Senior Contractor = hourly rate × hours/week × 48 working weeks/year.

Default benchmarks are directional, drawn from publicly reported US figures: executive base salaries scale roughly $165K–$270K+ from seed to growth stage (compensation surveys such as Built In and Kruze Consulting); benefits/payroll overhead runs ~40–45% of base in the US (Bureau of Labor Statistics); average executive cost-per-hire is reported around $36,000 (SHRM); and fractional CTO retainers in the US market typically fall between $8,000 and $25,000/month depending on scope and days/week (fractional-marketplace rate data, e.g. Fractionus, Go Fractional, A.Team). Every one of these is a benchmark, not a quote — adjust freely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the real difference between a fractional CTO and a contractor?

A contractor is typically scoped to a project or a deliverable. A fractional CTO holds the role itself — technical strategy, architecture decisions, team leadership, vendor and hiring calls, and accountability for outcomes — just on a part-time cadence instead of full-time. That's why the "what this doesn't measure" table above treats them very differently even when the dollar figures land close together.

How many days a week does a fractional CTO typically work?

Most US engagements run 1–3 days per week, sometimes expressed as a set number of hours or a fixed monthly retainer instead. Early-stage companies often start lighter (1 day/week) and increase cadence around fundraising, a major technical decision, or a team build-out.

When does it make sense to hire full-time instead?

Generally once the engineering org reaches a size and complexity where day-to-day presence, team management, and always-on accountability matter more than the cost savings — commonly somewhere around Series B/C, or earlier if technology is the core product. Many companies also use a fractional CTO as a bridge while running a full-time search, which is exactly the "cost of delay" scenario this calculator is built to model.

Are these numbers specific to my company?

No — they're US-market benchmarks meant to get you in the right order of magnitude quickly. Your actual compensation band depends on your location, industry, funding stage, and the specific candidate pool. Swap in your own numbers wherever you have them.

Do you store what I enter?
